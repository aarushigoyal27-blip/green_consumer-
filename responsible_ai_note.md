# Responsible AI Note – PS1

## 1. Purpose and scope
The system finds customers who may be interested in four green products (EV Finance, Green Home Loan, Solar Finance, Paperless Banking), estimates the carbon impact, and explains the result in plain language. Users are customers (their own view) and business teams (aggregate dashboard). It must **not** be used to decide credit eligibility, to price or approve a loan, or to contact anyone who has opted out. Every output carries `eligibility: NOT_ASSESSED`.

## 2. Division of responsibility
| Component | Does | Must not |
|---|---|---|
| ML models | Predict propensity / scores | Decide eligibility |
| Carbon engine & calculators | Compute impact from documented formulas and factors | Be overridden by the LLM |
| LLM | Explain structured outputs | Predict, calculate, invent figures, decide eligibility |
| Eligibility rules | Set by product rules and authorised processes (`exclusion_engine.py`) | — |

## 3. Privacy
- Data used: **synthetic only**, generated by `generate_data.py` (seed 42). No real personal or confidential banking data is used anywhere.
- External LLM APIs (optional): only the structured result for one customer × product is sent (product, propensity, reason codes, impact numbers, a few usage figures). Customer name, address and free text are never sent; the customer ID is a synthetic code. Without an API key nothing leaves the app.
- Logging and retention: the app stores nothing about users. Generated data lives in the app's temporary file system and is rebuilt on restart.
- Marketing consent is enforced: an opt-out excludes the customer, and unknown consent is treated as not consented (never assumed).

## 4. Bias and fairness
- Features that may proxy protected traits: age band, income band, home ownership, region and property type. Age and income are used as model inputs.
- Check run (validation-style scoring of 600 customers, share of customers with a high-propensity offer, threshold 0.3):
  - Green Home: high income 22.1% vs low income 2.1%.
  - Solar: high income 4.1% vs low income 1.0%.
  - Paperless: age 60+ 4.6% vs 45–59 28.7%.
  - Renters and people living with family receive no Green Home or Solar offers (0%), by rule.
- Risk: lower-income customers, renters and older customers are offered green products less often. Part of this is by design (renters cannot install a roof or retrofit) and part is learned from the synthetic data, where income raises take-up.
- Mitigations: ownership exclusions are explicit and documented rather than hidden in a model; there is no upper age limit in the rules; paperless offers to older customers are suppressed only where the customer has no digital channel. Before real use the team should review thresholds by segment and consider non-credit options (energy-saving advice) for excluded groups.

## 5. Explainability
- Each recommendation shows: reason codes, propensity, confidence, the carbon method and assumptions, and the data cut-off date.
- Reason codes (fixed list): `HIGH_FUEL_SPEND` (monthly fuel £150+), `HIGH_ANNUAL_MILEAGE` (10,000 km+), `EXISTING_VEHICLE`, `OLDER_VEHICLE` (8+ years), `EXISTING_VEHICLE_LOAN`, `HIGH_ELECTRICITY_SPEND` / `HIGH_GAS_SPEND` (top quartile), `HOME_OWNER`, `HOME_IMPROVEMENT_SPEND`, `PAPER_STATEMENTS` (half or more paper), `HIGH_DIGITAL_ACTIVITY` (above median logins). They are set by simple threshold rules on the customer's data; they describe the customer, not the model's internal reasoning.
- Impact is labelled as an **estimate** with its method; ranges are shown where the engine gives them.
- Confidence is the share of model inputs that are present. It is a data-completeness measure, not a calibrated probability.

## 6. Hallucination and misuse controls
| Risk | Control | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| LLM invents numbers | Prompt receives structured JSON only; every number in the output is checked against it | 200 of 200 injected false figures caught; 200 of 200 reference explanations clean |
| LLM implies eligibility / guarantees | Prompt rules; payload says `NOT_ASSESSED` | prompt in `grounding.py` |
| Wrong or stale figure shown | Numbers come from the carbon engine, not the LLM; data date shown | carbon hand-checks 6 of 6 |
| Prompt injection via free text | No free text from customers or transactions reaches the prompt, only the structured fields | by design |

## 7. Human oversight
- Customers flagged vulnerable are marked `requires_human_review` and should be reviewed by a person before any outreach.
- Recommendations are suggestions for a relationship team, not automatic offers; the team reviews before outreach. Customers can ignore or decline any offer, and a decline starts a cooling-off period in the rules.
- Monitoring: recommended before real use: monthly review of take-up and recommendation rates by segment and a retrain when they drift.

## 8. Data limitations and known risks
- Data is synthetic. Labels follow a known formula plus noise, so reported accuracy overstates real-world performance.
- Consent, account status, compliance status, arrears and property type are not produced by the generator; the app fills demo defaults (consent given, active account, no holds, no arrears, semi-detached house). Real data must replace these.
- Emissions factors are UK Government 2026 factors in `emission.csv`; several inputs (solar yield, home-retrofit savings, grams of paper per statement) are documented team assumptions, and there is no hybrid factor, so hybrids return insufficient data.
- Estimated impacts are not causal and not guaranteed.
